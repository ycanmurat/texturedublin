New-build homes in Dublin, Meath and Kildare are often handed over with bare bedrooms, a basic kitchen and very little storage. Getting the order of work right saves time, money and a lot of boxes in the hall. Here is a simple checklist.

## Before you move in

- **Measure every room** — wall widths, ceiling heights, window and door positions, sockets and radiators. Photos help.
- **Find out where services run** — ask your builder for drawings showing pipes, cables and ventilation ducts before anything is fixed to the walls.
- **Know which walls are plasterboard** — most internal walls in new builds are plasterboard on studs, so heavy items need the right fixings or extra support.
- **Book made-to-measure work early** — fitted wardrobes typically take three to five weeks to make once the design is signed off.

## Week one: the essentials

Beds, a sofa, a table and somewhere for clothes. Flat-pack furniture can usually be assembled within days, and doing several rooms in one visit is the most efficient approach.

**Anchor tall furniture.** Wardrobes, bookcases and chests of drawers should be fixed to the wall with anti-tip brackets — especially in homes with children.

## The first months: built-in storage

- **Bedrooms:** fitted wardrobes that use the full ceiling height, with drawers and shoe storage inside.
- **Living room:** a media wall or TV unit with hidden cables, ideally before the walls are decorated.
- **Utility:** cabinets around a stacked washer and dryer, with a tall cupboard for cleaning equipment.
- **Hall:** a bench with shoe storage, or a coat cupboard under the stairs.

## Decorate in the right order

Ideally, built-in furniture goes in before the final coat of paint, so walls can be finished neatly around it. Floating shelves and wall-mounted TVs are best fitted once you know where the furniture will sit.

## Snags and warranties

Report snags to your builder before drilling into the areas concerned, and keep a note of where fixings go. If you are unsure how wall fixings are treated under your home's warranty, ask before the work starts.
