## Which type of sign suits your street?

- **Hand-painted timber and individual letters** suit heritage streets and conservation areas — think the Liberties, Ranelagh or Dalkey.
- **Aluminium panels and lightboxes** work well on suburban parades and shopping centres, where landlords often set fascia sizes.
- **Halo-lit letters** give a premium, discreet glow and are often the illuminated option planners are most comfortable with.
- **Projecting signs** catch passing foot traffic on busy pavements.

## Do I need planning permission?

Some small, non-illuminated signs on business premises can be **exempted development**, but the exemptions are narrow. As a rule of thumb you should expect to need planning permission if your sign is:

- **illuminated** in any way;
- a **projecting** sign or one above the ground-floor fascia;
- larger than a modest fascia name board;
- on a **protected structure** or inside an **Architectural Conservation Area**.

Applications go to your local council — Dublin City Council, Dún Laoghaire-Rathdown, Fingal or South Dublin County Council, or Wicklow, Kildare and Meath for commuter towns. Each of our area pages names the council for that area.

## What an application involves

A signage application normally needs drawings of the sign and shopfront, a photomontage, a site location map, a newspaper notice and a site notice, and the application fee. Councils usually decide within about eight weeks, followed by a short appeal period before you can proceed. We prepare drawings and photomontages from <Price s="shop-signs" i="planning" />.

Check your lease too: many landlords and shopping centres must approve signage before you apply.

## Electrical safety

Illuminated signs must be connected and certified by a **Safe Electric registered electrical contractor** in line with I.S. 10101. We build with CE-marked LED modules and drivers rated for outdoor use, and arrange the certified connection for you.
