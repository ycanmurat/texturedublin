## Getting the proportions right

The best alcove units look as if they were part of the original house. A few principles we follow:

- **Line up with the mantelpiece.** The top of the base cupboard usually sits level with — or just below — the mantel, so the room reads as one composition.
- **Keep base cupboards shallow.** Around 350–400 mm deep is plenty for most rooms and stops the units overpowering the chimney breast.
- **Space shelves for real life.** Floating shelves at 300–350 mm spacing suit books and objects; we vary the spacing slightly so the wall doesn't look like a grid.
- **Respect the details.** We stop units short of cornicing and picture rails, scribe them to skirting, and never cut into original features.

## Old walls are never straight

Victorian and Edwardian walls bow, lean and step. Off-the-shelf furniture leaves visible gaps; made-to-measure units are **scribed** — cut precisely to the wall, floor and ceiling — on site, so they fit perfectly. That is why alcove units in period homes are almost always bespoke.

## Media walls and hidden cabling

A media wall turns the TV into part of the room rather than the focus of it. We plan concealed cable routes, ventilated cupboards for consoles and routers, recesses for soundbars or electric fires, and sockets where you actually need them. In newer homes without a chimney breast, a media wall creates the architecture a plain room lacks.

## Finishes

Most alcove units are **spray-painted** — colour-matched to your walls for a seamless look, or in a contrasting shade to make a feature. Solid oak or oak-veneer shelves add warmth. We can also deliver units primed and ready for your decorator.
