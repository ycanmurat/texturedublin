Kitchen doors set the character of the whole room. Three styles cover most of the kitchens we design: Shaker, handleless and slab. Each can be made in many colours and finishes, so the real question is which suits your home and the way you use your kitchen.

## Shaker: the timeless choice

A Shaker door has a flat centre panel framed by a simple square edge. It works almost anywhere — Victorian terraces, 1930s semi-detached houses and new builds alike — and is easy to dress up or down with handles and colour. The kitchen in the photo uses Shaker doors in two tones, with a timber-faced island as a contrast.

**Good to know:** the frame creates small ledges that gather a little more dust than a flat door. Painted Shaker doors in a satin finish are easy to wipe and can be touched up.

## Handleless: clean lines for modern rooms

Handleless kitchens use a recessed rail, sometimes called a J-pull or channel, instead of handles. The result is calm and architectural — ideal for open-plan extensions where the kitchen is visible from the living area.

**Good to know:** the rails must be planned precisely with the worktop and appliances, so this style rewards a measured design. Integrated appliances keep the lines uninterrupted.

## Slab: simple, versatile and practical

A slab door is a flat panel with no frame. With handles it can look Scandinavian, industrial or minimal depending on the finish — matt lacquer, woodgrain, timber veneer or a durable laminate. It is often the most cost-effective option and the easiest to clean.

## Colour and finish

- **Painted** doors can be matched to any colour and refreshed later.
- **Timber veneer** brings warmth, especially on islands and tall larders.
- **Laminate and woodgrain boards** are hard-wearing and practical for busy family kitchens and rental properties.

Two-tone kitchens — darker base units with lighter wall units, or a contrasting island — remain popular and help a large kitchen feel less heavy.

## Matching the style to the house

- **Period homes:** Shaker or in-frame doors respect the architecture.
- **Extensions and new builds:** handleless or slab doors suit large windows and open-plan spaces.
- **Apartments:** slab or Shaker doors in light colours make compact kitchens feel bigger.

## New doors on an existing kitchen

If your cabinets are sound, new made-to-measure doors and drawer fronts can change the style without replacing the whole kitchen. We check the hinges, runners and carcasses at the survey before recommending it.
