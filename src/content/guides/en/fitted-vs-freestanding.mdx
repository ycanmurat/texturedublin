Choosing between a fitted wardrobe and a freestanding one comes down to the room, how long you plan to stay and how much storage you need. Both have their place. Here is a straightforward comparison.

## Storage: fitted wins on capacity

A fitted wardrobe runs from floor to ceiling and from wall to wall, so it uses the space a freestanding unit leaves empty — the gap above it, the strip beside it and the corners. In a typical Irish bedroom with a 2.4 m ceiling, the extra height alone can add a full shelf of storage along the whole run.

Freestanding wardrobes come in fixed sizes. In a room that happens to match, they work well; in most rooms they leave gaps that collect dust.

## Awkward rooms favour made-to-measure

Sloped attic ceilings, chimney breasts, bay windows, bulkheads and uneven walls are where fitted furniture earns its keep. Each panel is scribed to the wall, floor and ceiling, so the wardrobe looks built in rather than squeezed in. Period homes benefit in particular, because very little in them is square.

## Cost: what drives the price

A good freestanding wardrobe costs less up front. A fitted wardrobe's price depends on the width and height, the doors (hinged or sliding), the finish and the interior — drawers, shoe racks and lighting all add to it. You get far more storage per wall, built to last, but it is a bigger investment. The only fair comparison is a written quote for your room.

## Renting, moving and resale

If you rent, or expect to move within a year or two, a freestanding wardrobe — or a modular system such as IKEA PAX, assembled and fitted properly — is the sensible choice, because it moves with you.

If you own your home, well-made fitted wardrobes are seen as a feature by many buyers, particularly in new builds where bedrooms are often handed over with no storage at all.

## Style and finish

Fitted doors can be painted in any colour, made in oak or walnut veneer, panelled to suit a period house or kept flat and handle-free for a modern look. Inside, the layout is designed around how you store clothes: long and short hanging, shelves, drawers, shoe racks and lighting.

## A middle route

Some homeowners choose a modular wardrobe system and have it installed between the walls with infill panels scribed to the ceiling. It gives a built-in look at a lower cost, although the interior and door options are limited to the system.

## Which should you choose?

- Choose **freestanding** if you rent, need a quick solution or have a room that suits standard sizes.
- Choose **fitted** if you own your home, want maximum storage or have sloped ceilings, alcoves or uneven walls.
