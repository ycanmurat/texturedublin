## Where the money goes

**Cabinetry and doors** are the heart of the cost. Door style makes a big difference: flat slab doors are the most economical, Shaker and handleless designs sit in the middle, and traditional in-frame kitchens — where doors sit inside a solid frame — take the most time to make and fit.

**Worktops** are the next big variable. Laminate is included in our guide prices. As a rough guide, solid timber adds around €1,200–€2,600 and quartz around €2,200–€5,200 for a typical family kitchen, depending on length, joints and cut-outs.

**Building work** — moving a sink, knocking a wall or adding sockets — is often what pushes a project over budget. We flag it at survey so there are no surprises.

## Dublin homes and kitchen layouts

In **Victorian and Edwardian red-bricks**, kitchens are often in a narrow rear return or a new extension. Efficient galley layouts suit returns; islands suit extensions. In **1930s–70s semis**, knocking the kitchen and dining room together creates space for an L-shape or island. **Apartments** benefit most from full-height storage and neatly integrated appliances. ## How long does it take?

Design and drawings usually take one to three weeks. Manufacture takes around 6–8 weeks from sign-off, and installation 4–10 working days depending on size. We plan the order of work so that you are without water and power for as little time as possible.
